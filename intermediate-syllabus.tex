\sub{Description}

This ceramics throwing course intends to further both technical skills
and to impart clear analytical practices for iterating on and refining
work.  The core technical skills center on throwing efficiency and
ergonomics. Lastly, we set aside time for special techniques that
students in the class wish to pursue. Optionally, we can schedule
outside class sessions for practical critique if the students wish to
do that.

\sub{Calendar}

\begin{multicols}{2}

	\split{Session 1}{Centering, Opening / Drawing}{6/29 7:30-9:30pm}
	\begin{itemize}
		\item \textbf{Drawing}: Using the paper sheets passed out in class, draw a
		      mug silhouette inside the 4x5 in box. Cut out the better half of your
		      silhouette and paste it onto a piece of cardboard. Then cut out the mug
		      portion of your drawing to make a form tool.
		\item \textbf{Centering}: We cover centering short and tall. For practice,
		      center 1.5 to 3lbs, knock of center, recenter without adding water, and
		      repeat until failure. Focus on how the stickiness of the clay changes with
		      less water.
		\item \textbf{Opening Short}: Center clay into a puck, dig down about 1 cm
		      and open into short walls, dig down another 1 cm and open into short walls
		      again, continue until you get to the wheel head. Focus on cleanly
		      blending the opened clay into the existing walls.
		\item \textbf{Opening Tall}: Center clay into a flat topped cone. Slowly
		      drill down into the center with your left thumb while lightly controlling
		      the walls splaying out with your right hand. Notice how it is easier when
		      you move more slowly and pay attention to clay stickiness.
	\end{itemize}

	\split{Session 2}{Pulling, Shaping / Timing}{7/13 7:30-9:30pm}
	\begin{itemize}
		\item \textbf{Pulling}: Knuckle Pull, Collar Pull, Compression Pull.
		\item \textbf{Shaping}: The fundamentals of using ribs for shaping.
		\item \textbf{Timing}: Prepare 10 balls of clay each weighing ~1lb, start a
		      five minute timer with the intention to throw the mug shape made in
		      Session 1, when the time goes off wire off the piece even if it is not
		      finished and cut it in half to check wall thickness. Rest for 1ish minute
		      between pots but don't do any analysis until all 10 balls have been
		      thrown. Notice how throwing is easier as you focus only on throwing
		      techniques and not on what the outcome is.
	\end{itemize}

	\split{Session 3}{Trimming, Handles}{6/20 7:30-9:30pm}
	\begin{itemize}
		\item \textbf{Wet Chucks}: Making and using a wet chuck for bottles, cups,
		      and bowls.
		\item \textbf{Trimming}: Using counter friction to trim quickly. Using a
		      wire tool to trim thick pieces.
		\item \textbf{Handles}: Pull several blanks (thick and thin). Attach to a
		      practice cylinder by blending or shaping. Pull and attach the tail.
		      Practice 4 handles to each practice cylinder.
	\end{itemize}

	\split{Session 4}{Throwing Larger}{6/27 7:30-9:30pm}
	\begin{itemize}
		\item \textbf{Centering}: Centering 3 to 5lbs of clay using a medium speed
		      and less strength.
		\item \textbf{Opening}: Using the tall opening method drill down until the
		      left hand is full of clay. If there is still more clay to go, use the left
		      fist to punch down into the clay and compress the base. Lastly, use the
		      flat outside of the left thumb and the right palm to lightly compress and
		      even the walls.
		\item \textbf{Big Pulls}: Thick pulls with the flat thumb and palm of
		      outside hand. Collar pulls to get initial height. Big knuckle pulls for
		      initial height.
		\item \textbf{}:
	\end{itemize}

	\columnbreak

	\split{Session 5}{Fundamentals: Chucks \& Attachments}{7/3 7:30-9:30pm}
	\begin{itemize}
		\item \textbf{Techniques}: Throwing and altering a wet chuck. Handles, Lugs, and Buttons.
		\item \textbf{Analysis}: Observing surface: defining color, texture, and gloss.
		\item \textbf{Exercises}: Make a cup chuck (11cm base, 7cm top, 12cm tall).
	\end{itemize}

	\split{Session 6}{Fundamentals: Glazing}{7/10 7:30-9:30pm}
	\begin{itemize}
		\item \textbf{Techniques}: Dipping glaze. Pouring glaze. Painting with slip. Waxing.
		\item \textbf{Analysis}: Putting it all together with composition.
		\item \textbf{Exercises}: Make a cup chuck (11cm base, 7cm top, 12cm tall).
	\end{itemize}

	\split{Session 7}{Special Topics: Q\&A}{7/17 7:30-9:30pm}
	At this point we have covered all of the fundamental topics. Students are
	encouraged to come with questions on additional forms or technique that either
	weren't covered or they wish to delve into deeper. If there are no such
	questions or we end up with a lull then we will all make teapots.
